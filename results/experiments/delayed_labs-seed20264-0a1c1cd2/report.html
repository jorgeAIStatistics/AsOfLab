Chronological evaluation

How would a model built from what was known at the cutoff date have performed on the patients who arrived afterwards?

The patient holdout asks about new patients from the same period. This asks about later patients. The simulation has no drift over calendar time, so no systematic difference between the two is expected here; the design is shown because real data would need it.

Cutoff: 2025-05-01T00:00:00+00:00.

  • A prediction is used for fitting only if its label was mature at the cutoff: prediction time + horizon + maximum ascertainment lag <= cutoff.
  • Fitting labels are computed from outcomes ascertained by the cutoff.
  • Test predictions are those after the cutoff from patients first seen after the cutoff.
  • A patient seen before the cutoff who returns afterwards is excluded from the test set.

Methods and limits

Methods

  • Uncertainty: 2,000 bootstrap resamples of patients (seed 20261013), percentile intervals at the 95% level. A patient drawn more than once contributes all of its predictions that many times. The same resamples are used for every condition, and each difference is formed within a resample.
  • A statistic is reported without an interval when fewer than 95% of the resamples could be evaluated. The number completed is recorded for every estimate in statistics.json.
  • Models: logistic regression with median imputation, missing-value indicators and standardisation, all fitted on training patients; the regularisation strength was chosen on validation patients. Test patients were used once.

Limits

  • All data are simulated. Nothing here is evidence about any real patient, hospital, or model.
  • The size and direction of every difference depend on the simulation's settings, which were fixed in advance and are not estimates of any real system.
  • Bootstrap intervals describe sampling variability in the test patients for models held fixed. They exclude the variability of model fitting and of the simulation itself.
  • A lower score under the as-known policy does not mean a worse engine. It means the naive number was measuring something the deployed pipeline could not have had.
  • Timestamp rules cannot establish clinical appropriateness, causality, or data validity.
  • Subgroup results are descriptive. They support no claim about fairness, safety, or benefit.
